Charging Safety Week — Day 6: Designing a Warning-Signs Mechanic Kids Actually Remember

For Day 6 of Charging Safety Week, the Fire Babys design question was: how do you teach "don't touch it" in a game built around touching things? The answer is a spot-the-signs poster mechanic — Blaze, Ruko & Cinder study five icons (thermometer, swollen battery, strange smell, hissing, smoke) and the player wins by choosing the safe response: step back, tell a grown-up, go outside. Interacting with the device itself is the one move the game never rewards.

That inversion matters for kid-safe design. The fantasy stays empowering — kids are the heroes who noticed first — while the real-world action stays exactly what firefighters ask for: no handling, no unplugging, just alert an adult and exit.

The pros: "If the smoke alarm sounds, GET OUT AND STAY OUT. Never go back inside for people or pets. If you have to escape through smoke, GET LOW AND GO under the smoke to your way out. Call the fire department (911) from outside your home." — National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) (Source: https://www.longisland.com/news/01-06-17/nfpa-cpsc-fire-safety-challenge-toolkit.html)
