Where Your Smoke Alarms Should Actually Be (Most Homes Get This Wrong)

One alarm in the hallway isn't enough. Fire-safety pros recommend a smoke alarm inside every bedroom, outside each sleeping area, and on every level of your home — basement included. Interconnect them if you can, so when one sounds, they all sound.

Maintenance is the unglamorous part that saves lives: test every alarm monthly with the test button, and replace the whole unit every 10 years (check the manufacture date on the back). A chirping alarm isn't annoying — it's asking for help. Answer it the same day.

And when one sounds for real, the U.S. Fire Administration's rule is the whole plan: "Get out fast and stay out. Never go back inside for people, pets or things. Smoke is poisonous. If you must escape through smoke, get low and go under the smoke to your exit." (Source: https://www.safeoutcome.com/documents/00120/USFA%20Escape%20Planning.pdf)
