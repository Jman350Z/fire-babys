Two Ways Out Saturday — Why Every Room Needs a Backup Exit

One exit is a plan with no backup. Fire safety experts teach two ways out of every room because the usual way — the door — can be the very way that's blocked by heat or smoke. A window, a second door, or a fire-escape ladder gives your family a second chance that is decided before an emergency, not during one.

Start simple: draw your home, mark both exits in every room, and walk both routes to your outside meeting place. Feel every door before opening it during a drill — a hot door means that route is closed and the second way is now the plan.

The pros: "In a fire: get low and go. Feel doors before opening — if a door is hot, use your second way out. Once you're out, stay out. Never go back inside for belongings or pets. Call 911 from outside." — Plainfield Fire Protection District, IL (Source: https://www.pprfd.com/fire-safety-planning.html)
