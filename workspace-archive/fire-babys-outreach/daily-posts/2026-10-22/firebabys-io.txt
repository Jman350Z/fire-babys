Why we teach the beep — and the crawl beneath it

Every Fire Babys lesson starts from a real firefighter rule, and today's is pure physics: smoke rises. Heat and smoke climb to the ceiling first, which means the air a person can still breathe during an escape is the air near the floor. "Get low and go" is not a slogan; it is the geometry of survival, taught the same way in fire stations across the country.

That is why today's concept art shows Blaze, Ruko & Cinder doing the unglamorous, life-saving thing: down on hands and knees, under a pretend-smoke band, checking a door, and crawling a clear hallway to an open door and a meeting-place tree. Practice turns panic into muscle memory — for everyone, at every age.

The National Fire Protection Association's rule frames the whole drill: "If the smoke alarm sounds, GET OUT AND STAY OUT. Never go back inside for people or pets. If you have to escape through smoke, GET LOW AND GO under the smoke to your way out. Call the fire department (911) from outside your home." — National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) (Source: https://www.longisland.com/news/01-06-17/nfpa-cpsc-fire-safety-challenge-toolkit.html)

Fire Babys games teach fire safety through play for everyone. Game proceeds are intended to fund real U.S. fire-department school programs one day — no partnerships exist yet; that's the mission we're building toward.
