# THE SLIMERS — Character Spec (Fire Babys villain)

Created at Jeff's order, 2026-10-17: "add the new bad guy slimers — they are an oil
based bad guy so it's harder to beat him unless you know the secret to stopping
oil based fires."

Status: DESIGN/SPEC ONLY. Not yet in any game code. Game integration is sequenced
after the language system and the full game audit.

## WHO THEY ARE

The Slimers are oil-creatures — living blobs of glossy black-and-purple oil who
drip, ooze, and slide everywhere they go, leaving a rainbow slick trail behind
them. They are the Fire Babys' slippery new troublemakers: goofy-menacing,
kid-safe scary-funny, in the same silent slapstick register as the skeleton bad
guys. A Slimer doesn't stomp or charge — he SLOSHES. He seeps under doors,
puddles across floors, and gloats while everybody slips around him.

- **Personality:** smug, giggly, overconfident. He thinks he's unbeatable
  because most things heroes throw at him just make him bigger (see THE SECRET).
  His whole act falls apart the second somebody covers him up — then he's all
  muffled panic and wobbling.
- **Characters-are-gold rule applies in full:** a Slimer NEVER harms a Fire
  Baby, never hurts anyone, and is never the vehicle for a joke at a Baby's
  expense. The joke is always on the Slimer — especially when he gets smothered
  and deflates into a sad little puddle. Babys win by being SMART, not by
  overpowering him.
- **Movement:** slow, oozy, squash-and-stretch sloshing. He travels by
  puddle-gliding, stretching thin to squeeze through gaps, and plopping back
  into a blob. Everything he touches gets a shiny rainbow oil slick.

## THE SECRET — how you stop an oil fire (the teaching core)

This is REAL fire safety, and it's the Slimers' whole game mechanic:

- **Water does NOT work on oil.** Spraying a Slimer with water makes him
  **SPLASH and SPREAD** — he spatters into drips, the puddle gets WIDER, and
  he comes back BIGGER, giggling. In-game: water-type attacks fail or
  backfire. Aqua's hose is the trap choice — the obvious move is the wrong
  move. This teaches the real rule: **never put water on a grease/oil fire —
  it spreads it.**
- **The way to beat him is to SMOTHER him.** Oil fire needs air; take the air
  away and it dies. In-game, smother-type attacks WIN:
  - **Lid drop** — clap a big pot lid / cover over him.
  - **Foam blanket** — bury him under fire-foam.
  - **Sand dump** — pour sand over the slick.
  - **Baking soda** — dump baking soda on a small Slimer.
  Covered, he panics, shrinks, and deflates into a harmless little puddle
  with dizzy spiral eyes.
- **Turn off the heat.** If there's a burner/stove/heat source feeding the
  scene, switching it OFF (a grown-up move / a clearly marked switch in-game)
  weakens him and stops him regrowing. Sequence the lesson: heat off, then
  smother.
- **Never carry a burning pan, never fan the flames, get a grown-up.** The
  Slimer encounter always ends with the safety line (below).

### Game mechanic summary (for integrators)

| Attack type | Result vs. Slimer |
|---|---|
| Water / spray / hose | FAILS — he splashes, spreads, splits into drips, comes back bigger |
| Fire / heat | FAILS — he's oil; heat feeds him, he flares up smugly |
| Lid / cover drop | WINS — smothers him, he shrinks |
| Foam blanket | WINS — smothers him fast |
| Sand dump | WINS — smothers and soaks the slick |
| Baking soda (small Slimers) | WINS — smothers small ones |
| Turning off the heat source first | Weakens him; smother attacks work better/faster after |

Player-facing tell: when a wrong attack hits, he wobbles happily and grows —
kids should READ that as "that's not working, think!" When covered, he panics
and shrinks — that's the "you found the secret" moment.

## DESIGN — for artists and generators

- **Body:** one round, glossy oil-blob body — dark iridescent black-purple
  with slow rainbow oil-slick swirls (purple, teal, magenta, gold sheen)
  moving across the surface. The blob IS the body, like the Babys' ball
  bodies — no human torso, no human proportions, no legs.
- **Arms:** two big drippy stub arms made of the same oil, ending in melty
  blob hands. They drip constantly.
- **Face:** sits directly on the blob — big expressive eyes (sly half-lidded
  gloat by default) and a wide, drippy, smug mouth. When panicked: eyes go
  wide and round, mouth becomes a small wobbly "o". When smothered/defeated:
  dizzy spiral eyes, flat deflated puddle shape.
- **Trail:** leaves a shimmering rainbow oil slick wherever he moves.
- **Sizes:** regular Slimers are about Fire-Baby size. A splash-split makes
  mini-drip Slimers (same design, smaller, squeakier). A "fed" Slimer (after
  absorbing spray) puffs up bigger and glossier for a beat before settling.
- **Style lock:** bold ink outlines, plushie-toyetic chunky shapes, vibrant
  Saturday-morning cartoon, squash-and-stretch slapstick — matching the
  existing character brief.
- **Bans:** NO text, NO words, NO letters, NO numbers, NO signs, NO logos,
  NO emblems, NO real-world brand marks anywhere on the character or in
  art of him. No beaks/wings/feathers (he's a blob, not a bird). Nothing
  genuinely frightening — he's a goofy menace, not a horror monster. Never
  shown harming a Fire Baby or anyone else.

## HOW HE SHOWS UP

- **Games (planned, post-audit integration):** a special enemy type. The
  level teaches the secret by experience: spraying makes him spread, and
  the winning tools (lid, foam, sand) are the level's featured pickups.
  First appearances should include one safe "practice" Slimer so kids
  learn water backfires without punishment.
- **Videos:** one gag per video, wordless slapstick. The beat: hero reaches
  for the hose — Slimer GRINS (he wants the spray) — hero stops, thinks,
  grabs a LID (or foam/sand) instead — Slimer panics, gets covered, deflates
  into a dizzy puddle. The spray-backfire version (sprayed → splashes
  bigger → hero scrambles for the lid) is the comedy version of the same
  lesson. Babys are never harmed and never the butt of the joke; the Slimer
  is.
- **Safety line (house style, pairs with every Slimer appearance):**
  **"Oil fire? Never spray it — smother it, and get a grown-up."**
  (In the family of "Real fire is never a toy. Always get a grown-up.")
